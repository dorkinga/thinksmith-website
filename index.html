The five-part journey

Every challenge follows the same path

Whether a student is exploring a topic from curiosity or working through a course syllabus, the thinking process is always the same five parts. Ms. Rivera, the AI teacher, is available throughout every part — to review their work, answer questions, or simply help them find their own thinking.

1
Write your own thinking
Before seeing anything else. Raw and honest.
2
Check peer thinking
Three AI peers at different quality levels. Read and give specific feedback.
3
Receive peer feedback
The peers respond to your thinking. Write your reflection.
4
Revise if needed
Return to your solution with new understanding. Peers regenerate.
5
Thinking Outcome
Evidence-based competency scores built from your own writing.
For parents

Not a grade. A genuine picture of how they think.

The Thinking Outcome is not a score for getting the right answer. It is a competency assessment built entirely from evidence in your child's own writing — with direct quotes.

  • ✓ Five competency scores with evidence quotes from their actual writing
  • ✓ A personal narrative summary of how their thinking developed
  • ✓ A growth trajectory across all four parts of the journey
  • ✓ Downloadable as a PDF to share with instructors

Low early scores are normal and expected. They mean the assessment is honest.

Ms. Rivera — AI Teacher

She asks questions. She never gives answers.

ThinkSmith is built differently to the AI tools that have caused concern. Ms. Rivera is not a chat AI, a companion, or a counsellor. Every response she gives is constrained, structured, and tied to the learning activity — she cannot wander, improvise emotionally, or go beyond her role.

If your child shows distress

Ms. Rivera immediately stops all academic feedback and responds with care, directing your child to speak with a trusted adult. She does not escalate or send any alert to you or any authority.

ThinkSmith is not a crisis centre

Ms. Rivera responds with care in the moment — but does not contact parents or authorities. If your child is struggling, please maintain open conversations at home.

I don't get why compound interest grows so much faster. Can you just explain it?
You've already started to explain it in your own writing — you mentioned that interest gets added to the total each year. What do you think would happen if that total kept getting bigger every year?
